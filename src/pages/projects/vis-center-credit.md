Interactive exhibits are made by many people, including researchers, designers, developers, educators, technicians and museum staff, but visitors rarely see who they are. This project began with a practical question at the Visualization Center in Norrköping: how to credit everyone who contributes to an interactive exhibit. It turned out to be much harder than it sounds. We interviewed people working in science museums about how they think about contribution and recognition: who gets credited, who doesn’t, and how institutions make those decisions. The findings show that crediting is a socio-technical negotiation shaped by authorship, hierarchy, invisible labour and institutional culture. They also show that credit shapes how science is presented to the public, since leaving out the people behind an exhibit makes science look like something that simply exists. The work has resulted in a paper at IEEE VIS 2026, and it also feeds into our broader research on visualization in science museums.
